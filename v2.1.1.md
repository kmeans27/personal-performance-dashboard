# v2.1.1 — the density pass

**Status: shipped.** `2.1.1`

v2.1 gave every field the right *shape*. v2.1.1 gives every field the right
*size*, and gives the space it saves back to the day.

The problem, in the user's words: on a 16:9 screen "everything is just too big".
The board stretched itself to the height of the window and shared that height
between its rows, so a yes/no — two words and a switch — was handed a box the
size of a shoebox. Empty space *inside* a box is waste; empty space *around* a
box is breathing room. This pass removes the first and keeps the second.

---

## 1. Nothing stretches to fill the screen any more

The board used to be `height: calc(100dvh - chrome)` with `fr` rows. Few rows,
big window, giant tiles.

Now the board is **exactly as tall as its content**. It sits centred in whatever
the window gives it, with the leftover pixels left as page. The one length that
drives everything is a single row:

```css
--row: clamp(66px, 8.4dvh, 94px);
```

Everything else — the tile padding, the gaps, the chart heights — is derived
from that row, so the board scales with the window without ever turning a small
field into a big box. `container-type: size` came off the board (size
containment was making the board measure its own height as zero); tiles keep
their own containment, so `cqh`-based charts still shrink with their tile.

Above 1100px the board caps its own width at 1512px and centres, so a 27" screen
buys margins, not bigger boxes.

## 2. A field is as big as what it holds

Importance no longer buys space. **Shape does.**

Two fields earn a second cell:

- a **switch with a written note** (Learned) — the note is half the field;
- a **number with a track** (Sleep, Screen time) — a gauge needs a line to sit on.

Everything else is exactly one cell: label, number, shape, and one line of
meaning. `Tile` now carries `w` and `h` (grid columns and rows) instead of the
old abstract `size: 1|2|3`.

The three bands are three columns, and every band packs exactly — no band ends
on a half-empty row:

| Band | Fields | Cells | Rows |
| ---- | ------ | ----- | ---- |
| Body | 9 | 4 + 8×1 = 12 | 4 |
| Mind | 5 | 1 + 2 + 3×1 = 6 | 2 |
| Attention | 3 | 4 + 1 + 1 = 6 | 2 |

Eight rows in total. At 1440×900 a one-cell field is now **325×76** where v2.1
gave it 245×118: a flatter box that its content actually fills. A test asserts
that every band's cells divide by three, so the packing cannot silently rot.

## 3. The left third now says something

The rail used to be rings, a count and a one-line nudge. It is now what the day
*means*, top to bottom:

1. **The rings**, as before — the three categories around one score. They grow
   with the window up to a cap, because a bigger graphic is not a bigger box.
2. **The day**: date, "12 of 13 logged today", the three-stop meter, and one
   line — the next thing to try while the day is open, the closing line once it
   is complete. One slot, two messages.
3. **The three categories as bars.** This is the v0.1 overview the rings never
   quite replaced: Body / Mind / Attention, each with its score, reading left to
   right in the same accented colour as its arc and its band.
4. **This week** — a Mon→Sun strip, one bar per day, the bar being that day's
   score. Today is boxed. A day with no answer is an empty slot, never a zero.
   Every cell is a button: click a day and the day sheet opens.
5. **Three numbers**: the week's average, days logged (n of 7), best day.
6. **The two actions**: log everything, or go to the calendar.

The week comes from the API — `build_day_payload` now carries a `week` array
(Mon–Sun of the payload day's own week: `day`, `total`, `categories`, `logged`),
so the board no longer needs a second `/api/range` round trip to draw it. That
fetch is gone from `/` and `/demo`.

## 4. Tiles shed in a better order

A short tile used to keep its label, its number and a chart, then hide the chart.
Now it hides the *least useful thing first*, measured on its own content box:

| When the tile's content box is | It drops |
| --- | --- |
| under 44px | the caption line |
| under 36px | the chart |
| under 118px | the axis under a gauge, and the week spark (hero-only, again) |

Taller tiles gain things back in that order. The big two-row gauges are the only
tiles with room for the week spark and the axis, which is what makes them the
heroes of their bands.

## 5. Responsive

- **Above 1024px** — the two-column board: rail 27%, fields 73%, three columns.
- **701–1024px** — the rail lifts into a compact header band (rings, the day,
  then categories / week / numbers side by side), and the fields *keep three
  columns*: a field never gets wider than it needs just because it can.
- **Up to 700px** — the rail stacks, the fields go to two, and the first and
  last Mind tiles stretch so that band still ends on a full row.
- **Up to 460px** — one field per row.

Nothing scrolls on a desktop window; tablet and phone scroll the fields, which
is what a pocket screen is for.

## Tests

`uv run pytest` — **44 tests**, including the new band-packing and week tests.
Playwright harnesses in `/tmp` checked all seven pages at four viewports
(1920×1080, 1440×900, 1024×768, 390×844): no console errors, no horizontal
overflow, no clipped tile, and logging a field on the board still updates its
tile, its ring, the category bars and the count line in place.

## Known limits

- The night window's hour axis only appears when a tile is tall enough for it;
  on a 76px row the tile shows the window without its ruler.
- The week strip always shows the payload day's own week. Browsing a past week
  means the calendar page.
- Answering on the board is still [v2.2](v2.2.md) — this pass is layout only.
